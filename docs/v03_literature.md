# Related work and benchmark choices

## Wavefunction flows and the open implementation step

**Layden et al.** Sections III.1–III.2 map the continuity equation to Schrödinger evolution. For a real conservative field \(v_t=\nabla V_t\), the continuum generator is \([K,V_t]\), with \(K=-\tfrac12\Delta\), and the Hamiltonian is \(i[K,V_t]\). Section IV, Eqs. (24)–(29) and (36)–(41), gives Fourier collocation and the eight-factor commutator formula with four potential and four kinetic factors per step. Their notation is \(e^{i\beta D_{V_t}}\) for the potential factor and \(e^{i\alpha K}\) for the kinetic factor. The phase scale called \(\alpha\) in the general TT-oracle question is thus \(\beta\) in their product formula. Theorem 1 assumes periodic spatial smoothness through order \(2(s+1)\) for both \(\sqrt{p_t}\) and \(V_t\), integer \(s\ge(d+7)/4\), the stated temporal continuities, and the small-error condition \(\delta\le T\). Theorem 2 assumes continuous differentiability in time and gives a local \(O(\Delta t^2)\) spectral-norm bound for the formula frozen at the left endpoint. For midpoint snapshots one can add an independent time-freezing integral bound or establish a separate estimate. The paper's spatial theorem does not supply numerical values of the regularity constants for a learned checkpoint. The discussion after Eq. (34) excludes potential-oracle workspace from its system-qubit count. [Layden et al., arXiv:2510.08462v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08462v1)

**Kodama et al.** Sections 2.1–2.5 and Methods 4.3–4.12 report classical dense-grid and tensor-network wavefunction simulations, including a trained MPS potential that bypasses repeated tensor cross interpolation and acts in a TDVP evolution. The tensor-network state rank, potential rank, and observable rank play different roles. Their rare-event simulations use analytic amplification probabilities and do not charge a compiled coherent preparation. The reversible arithmetic sketch is in **Supplement S1.8, Proposition 2, Eq. (S16)**. It describes selected matrix multiply–adds followed by phase application and uncomputation. It gives \(O(dND_V^2)\) gates up to factors polynomial in \(b\) and describes an \(O(bD_V)\)-qubit arithmetic work register. Its estimate explicitly assumes negligible intermediate fixed-point rounding. It does not specify a garbage-free overwrite for a rectangular or singular matrix, a width policy, or a complete allocation and uncomputation schedule that realizes that work-register estimate. Treat the estimate as a construction-level model rather than a benchmark circuit. Proposition 1 already gives the diagonal phase Lipschitz bound for TT approximation. [Kodama et al., arXiv:2608.21700v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21700v1)

**Che and af Klinteberg.** Section 3.1.2, Fig. 3 and Eqs. (47)–(51), already implement the QROM load–phase–unload pattern. Sections 3.2 and 4.2–4.6 replace exact basis-word preparation with trained controlled-rotation encoders and compare selected accuracy and circuit resources. The QROM comparison uses the address-by-address multi-controlled-X construction of Sec. 2.4. Its measured cost does not establish the cost of unary iteration or SelectSwap. A probability-trained encoder also has a different certification obligation. For one address, if the encoder prepares \(\sum_z c_z|z\rangle\), a diagonal phase followed by the inverse encoder has clean amplitude \(\sum_z |c_z|^2e^{i\phi_z}\). The ancilla is exactly clean only when the phases agree on the nonzero support. A matched comparison would therefore need a uniform joint-state error check and comparable cost assumptions. [Che and af Klinteberg, arXiv:2610.11537v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11537v1)

## Established tools and resource comparators

- **TT approximation and conditioning.** Oseledets's Theorem 2.2 bounds the Frobenius-norm error of TT-SVD by the root sum of squared unfolding-tail bounds. A Frobenius bound also bounds the maximum entry error of the same tensor, usually conservatively. Canonicalization and the bond gauge \(A_jG,G^{-1}A_{j+1}\) preserve the exact represented tensor for invertible \(G\). They can change the size of individual selected slices, ranges, and errors after quantization. Orthogonality of a reshaped core does not imply that each absolute-entry matrix has norm at most one. Higham's Chapter 3 gives established componentwise product-error reasoning. Its floating-point relative-error model should not be substituted for the absolute grid-step errors of fixed-point arithmetic. [Oseledets, *Tensor-Train Decomposition*](https://doi.org/10.1137/090752286) · [Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9780898718027)
- **Reversible arithmetic and workspace.** Cuccaro et al. give linear-depth ripple-carry addition with one carry ancilla. The uniform precision compiler uses exact signed modular shift-and-add multiplication and preserves input registers, with a width argument needed before interpreting residues as signed values. Gidney's temporary logical AND reduces T count under a clean-target and measurement/feed-forward model. It cannot be substituted for every CCX in a unitary circuit count. Bennett's checkpointing and the Li–Tromp–Vitányi reversible pebble model formalize recomputation versus retained state. A TT cost depends on unequal register sizes and transition costs. A legal pebble removal requires an available predecessor and the inverse of the corresponding compute operation. [Cuccaro et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0410184) · [Gidney](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2018-06-18-74) · [Bennett](https://doi.org/10.1137/0218053) · [Li, Tromp and Vitányi](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9703009)
- **Strong lookup.** Babbush et al., Secs. III.1 and III.3, give a \(4M-4\) T forward unary-iteration lookup for \(M\) words using temporary ANDs. This is stronger than the cost of the benchmark's all-unitary exact-CCX shared-prefix lookup. Low, Kliuchnikov and Schaeffer, Sec. 2, Table 2 and Fig. 1, trade workspace for T gates with SelectSwap. The clean-bank lookup has leading terms \(4\lceil M/\lambda\rceil+8b\lambda\) for \(b\)-bit words and block size \(\lambda\). Their dirty-bank variant has different cost and restores unknown input. App. D.1.2 replaces the bitwise phase rotations with an adder into a phase-gradient state, which requires preparation and accounting for the additional register. These are forward-subroutine or implementation-model results. Complete phase-oracle comparisons must charge cleanup and phase implementation. [Babbush et al., arXiv:1805.03662v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.03662v2) · [Low, Kliuchnikov and Schaeffer, arXiv:1812.00954v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.00954v2)
- **Diagonal synthesis and rotation cost.** Parity-phase expansion is established diagonal synthesis. A truncated expansion can bound omitted angles by the sum of absolute omitted coefficients and must account for the global phase if the oracle is controlled. Ross–Selinger's typical \(3\log_2(1/\delta)+O(\log\log(1/\delta))\) concerns ancilla-free single-qubit \(Z\) rotations. A chosen finite offset in a T projection does not certify that a gate sequence exists at that cost. A proof budget for compiled rotations is conditional on the stated per-gate norm tolerance and phase convention, or on independent verification of actual synthesis. [Bullock and Markov](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0303039) · [Ross and Selinger, arXiv:1403.2975v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.2975v3)

## What this investigation can establish

The useful contribution is a reproducible answer to a specific implementation question. It applies componentwise fixed-point and range analysis to a reversible TT implementation, tests the arithmetic and workspace cleanup on learned data, and measures the complete accuracy–qubit–resource trade-offs against the competing circuits. The product formula, TT oracle, perturbation argument, lookup synthesis, and reversible checkpointing are established techniques. Any result favoring TT is relative to tested models, grids, arithmetic, resource assumptions, and lookup construction.

The smooth baseline model also admits a direct Fourier circuit. On its periodic grid each Fourier mode depends on one modular index \((k_1j_1+k_2j_2)\bmod N\), plus a known origin-dependent offset. A circuit can compute this index, apply a one-dimensional diagonal phase, and invert the index computation. The product of the per-mode phases realizes the same finite-grid potential. A comparison must charge the modular arithmetic, all selected mode rotations, scratch, and any offline coefficients or grouping. At fixed time, evaluating the time MLP classically is the same access convention used to instantiate TT cores. A classical representation with fewer terms does not by itself order the quantum resources.
